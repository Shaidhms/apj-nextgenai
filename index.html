Part 0 · Foundations

The 90-second AI primer

Before we open any tool, four words you'll keep hearing — AI, ML, DL, Gen AI. They're not the same thing. Knowing the difference makes you sound less lost in an AI conversation.

🧠
AI
The whole field — making machines do things that normally need human intelligence.
📊
ML
A way to do AI — let the machine learn patterns from data instead of coding rules by hand.
🕸️
DL
A flavour of ML using deep neural networks — what powers vision, speech, translation.
✨
Gen AI
DL that creates — text, images, music, code. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Suno all live here.
ℹ️
The onion analogyAI is the outer skin. ML is a layer inside. DL is a layer inside ML. Gen AI is the newest layer inside DL. Every tool in this guide sits in that innermost layer.

Most-used AI assistants today

You don't need to learn all of them. Here's the lay of the land so you know which one to reach for.

AssistantBest atWatch out for
ChatGPTEveryday chat, writing, coding, images via DALL·EKnowledge cutoff — invents facts when unsure
ClaudeLong careful thinking, code, document analysisNo live web in the free chat by default
GeminiGoogle integration, music (Lyria), video (Veo)Worse at long code than Claude
PerplexityResearch with citations, current eventsNot for creative writing or coding
SunoFull songs with vocals & lyricsFree tier — Suno owns the songs (no commercial use)
💡
Rule of thumbNeed a fact? Perplexity. Need to think? Claude. Need to make? Gemini / Suno. We'll use all of them today.
Part 1 · Perplexity

Your research co-pilot

Perplexity is not Google. It's not ChatGPT. It's a search engine that thinks, cites, and explains — built for people who need to back up what they say. Every answer comes with footnoted sources you can click.

100M+ monthly users Free tier covers most student work Pro $5/mo with student verification

Sign up & first search

1

Open perplexity.ai

Go to perplexity.ai in any browser. No app needed.

2

Sign in with Google

Top-right → Sign in → continue with Google. Free tier starts immediately.

3

Type a question — not a keyword

Perplexity thinks in full questions. Try "What's the difference between mitosis and meiosis?" — not "mitosis meiosis".

Three modes · three superpowers

Same Perplexity, three completely different ways to ask.

🔎
Search Mode
Quick answers with citations. Beats Google for homework — every fact has a clickable source for your reference list.
🧪
Deep Research
Returns a 3,000-word draft with 50+ sources. Free tier gets 5/day — perfect for board projects.
📖
Learn Mode
Step-by-step tutor that quizzes you and re-explains the bits you missed. Great the night before a test.
💡
Pro tipAlways say your level — "I'm in Class 11" or "explain at board exam level". Perplexity matches the depth to where you are. Don't ask like an adult; ask like a student.

Deep Research — full walk-through

1

Toggle Deep Research

Below the search box, click the Deep Research chip so it lights up.

2

Ask a real research question

Try "Detailed report on CRISPR gene editing for my Class 12 Biology project". The more specific the audience, the better the report.

3

Watch it work

It runs 50+ web searches in front of you — reading, comparing, summarising. Takes 2-3 minutes.

4

Export

Top of the report → Export → PDF, Word, or Markdown. Always cite Perplexity if you use it directly in submitted work.

So why not just use ChatGPT?

Same question — completely different answers. Here's why it matters when you're writing for marks.

PerplexityChatGPT & Claude
Live web?Yes — every query searches the webNo (by default) — has a knowledge cutoff
Citations?Mandatory — every fact has a footnoteNone — you must take its word
Hallucinations?Rare — sources keep it honestCommon — confident-sounding made-up facts
Best forResearch, current events, fact-findingWriting, coding, brainstorming, ideas
⚠️
The trapIf you copy a "fact" from ChatGPT into a board project without checking, you might be writing made-up information with full confidence. Use Perplexity for facts; Claude/ChatGPT for everything else.
Part 2 · Gemini · Music

Make a soundtrack in 30 seconds

Google's Lyria 3 is built right into the Gemini app. No signup beyond Google. No DAW. No music theory. Just a prompt, and 30 seconds later you have an MP3.

Free · 10 tracks/day 30-sec clip on free 3-min full track on AI Plus / Pro

Step 1 — Open Gemini

1

Visit gemini.google.com

Use the same Google account you use for Gmail. There's also a Gemini app for Android & iOS — same account works.

Step 2 — Tools → Create music

1

Open the Tools menu

Below the text box you'll see a small ⚙ Tools pill. Click it.

2

Pick "Create music"

You'll see options like Deep Research, Create music, Create image, Create video. Pick Create music.

3

Optional — drop in a mood reference

You can attach a photo or short video. Gemini will match the music's mood to the image — useful for soundtracks under specific scenes.

Step 3 — Prompt · generate · download

1

Write a vivid prompt

Be intent-based, not vague. "Lo-fi sunset chillhop, 80 BPM, piano lead, soft vinyl crackle, Chennai monsoon mood" beats "chill song" every time.

2

Pick the model

Fast = 30-sec clip (free). Thinking / Pro = 3-min full track (paid). Free is plenty for class assignments.

3

Generate · listen · download

Hit Generate. ~30 seconds later you get a track with cover art. Click the download icon → MP4 (with art) or MP3 (audio only).

🎯
Prompting cheat sheetAlways include — genre (lo-fi, indie folk, EDM), tempo (slow, 80 BPM, fast), instruments (piano, acoustic guitar, synth), mood (melancholy, hopeful, energetic), use case (intro music, study, gym).

Part 3 · Suno

Full songs with vocals

Where Gemini gives you a soundtrack, Suno writes you a song — lyrics, vocals, instrumentation, the lot. Real human-sounding singers in any genre, any language.

Step 1 — Sign up at suno.com

1

Visit suno.com

No download. Works in any browser, including phones.

2

Sign in

Continue with Google, Discord, or email. Free plan starts immediately — no card required.

Step 2 — Pick a mode · describe it

Suno has two modes. Pick based on how much control you want.

⚡
Simple mode
One field — describe the song in ~200 characters. Suno writes the lyrics for you.
🎚
Custom mode
Three fields — your own lyrics, a style string, and a title. Toggle vocals on/off.
📝
Use structure tags in lyricsWrap sections in tags so Suno knows the song structure: [Verse] [Chorus] [Bridge] [Outro]. Without these, you get a flat melody.

Step 3 — Generate · play · share

1

Hit Create

Each prompt drops 2 versions in your library. Listen to both — pick the one that lands.

2

Extend or remix

Right-click any track → Extend (add another verse), Remaster (re-render with the latest model), or Cover (same lyrics, different style).

3

Download / share

Track menu → Download MP3 or Share link. Public links anyone can play, no login.

Who owns the music?

Pro$10/mo~500Yes — you own it

⚖️
Free = personal use onlySongs you generate on the free tier belong to Suno. You cannot upload them to Spotify or use them in monetised YouTube videos. Need that? Upgrade to Pro — $10/mo, full commercial license.

Part 4 · Claude.ai

Meet Claude

Claude is an AI assistant built by Anthropic — known for safe, honest, careful long-form thinking. As of today it's #1 on coding benchmarks and the developer community's favourite for serious work.

Made by Anthropic #1 on SWE-Bench coding Free tier · ~10 messages every 5 hrs

Two flavours you'll actually use

💬
Claude.ai — Chat & Projects
The browser app at claude.ai. Upload docs, write, research, analyse, collaborate in shared Projects. For writers, analysts, everyday work.
⌨️
Claude Code
An agent that lives on the web (and in your terminal). Reads a repo, edits multiple files, runs tests, commits. For builders, developers, tinkerers. We'll use this next.

Sign in to claude.ai

1

Open claude.ai

Any browser. Click Sign up (or Sign in if you already have an account).

2

Continue with Google

Same Google account is fine. You'll get a verification code on your phone the first time.

3

Land on the chat

Big input box, file upload paperclip on the left, model picker top-left. The defaults are perfect for everyday use.

Projects — the killer feature

A Project is a shared workspace where you give Claude background context once — uploaded files, instructions, style — and every chat inside the project automatically uses it.

1

Create a Project

Left sidebar → Projects → + Create project. Name it (e.g. "My resume website").

2

Add knowledge

Drop in PDFs, docs, images. Resume, brand guidelines, sample writing — anything Claude should treat as background context for every chat in this project.

3

Set custom instructions

Tell Claude how to behave inside this project — "You're helping me build a personal portfolio. Always reply in friendly, plain English. Use Tailwind CSS in code."

→ claude.ai

Part 5 · Claude Code (Web)

Build a landing page from your resume

Claude Code is Anthropic's coding agent. The web version at claude.com/code lets you build, preview, and download full websites — without ever opening a terminal. We'll feed it your resume and get a personal landing page back.

claude.com/code No setup · runs in browser Needs Claude Pro or higher

Step 1 — Open Claude Code on the web

1

Go to claude.com/code

Same Claude account works. If you've only ever used claude.ai for chat, this is a different surface — same login, different tool.

2

Start a new session

Click + New session. You get a chat panel on the left and a live preview pane on the right.

Step 2 — Attach your resume

1

Click the paperclip

Bottom of the chat box. File picker opens.

2

Pick your resume

PDF, Word, or even an image of your CV — all work. Claude can read text from images too.

3

Optionally add a photo

Drop in a profile photo too — Claude can wire it into the hero section automatically.

Step 3 — Prompt the landing page

This is the whole secret — a clear, specific prompt. Use this one as a starter (edit the bracketed bits):

Sample prompt — copy & editI've attached my resume. Build me a personal landing page as a single index.html file (HTML + CSS + JS in one file, no framework, no build step). Sections: 1. Hero with my name, role, one-line tagline pulled from my resume 2. About — 2-3 short paragraphs in a friendly first-person voice 3. Experience — clean timeline of my last 3 roles 4. Skills — chip cloud of my top 8 skills 5. Featured projects — 3 cards (use placeholders if my resume has none) 6. Contact — email + LinkedIn buttons Style: - Dark theme, modern, generous whitespace - Plus Jakarta Sans + Inter fonts from Google Fonts - Single accent colour: warm orange (#ff5d3a) - Smooth scroll, sticky top nav - Mobile-first responsive Keep all images as inline SVG or external URLs — no separate files. Output the full index.html in a single code block I can copy.
🎯
Why this prompt worksIt tells Claude — (1) the input (resume), (2) the output (single index.html), (3) the structure (numbered sections), (4) the style (dark, accent, fonts), and (5) the constraints (one file, no build). Vague prompts get vague websites.

Step 4 — Iterate & download

Claude rarely nails it on the first try — and that's fine. Iterate by chatting.

1

Refine in plain English

Try follow-ups like — "Make the hero bigger and add my profile photo", "Change the accent to teal", "Add a testimonials row above contact".

2

Preview live

The right pane re-renders after every change. Resize the preview to see the mobile layout.

3

Download index.html

Top-right of the preview → Download, or copy the code block from the chat into a new file called index.html. That single file is your website.

Step 5 — Save it as index.html on your computer

This is the bridge between Claude and GitHub. The file on your computer is what we'll upload in Part 6.

1

If you clicked Download

It lands in your Downloads folder. Check the filename is exactly index.html — rename if needed (right-click → Rename on Windows, F2 on Mac).

2

If you copied the code instead

Open Notepad (Windows) or TextEdit (Mac, set to Plain Text mode: Format → Make Plain Text). Paste. Save as index.html. Make sure the dropdown says "All Files" — not "Text Document" or you'll get index.html.txt.

3

Double-click the file to test it

It opens in your browser as a local website. If your landing page looks right here, it'll look right on GitHub Pages.

💾
Save it as exactly index.htmlLowercase. No spaces. No .txt at the end. GitHub Pages looks for that exact filename — anything else won't load as the homepage.
➡️
Next up — push it to GitHubYou now have index.html on your computer. The next two parts take it from your laptop to a live URL on the internet.
Part 6 · GitHub

Push your project to GitHub

GitHub is the world's biggest place to store code & websites. Free forever for public projects. Your index.html goes here, and from here it gets a live URL.

Step 1 — Sign up · sign in

1

Open github.com

Click Sign up top-right.

2

Pick a username carefully

This becomes part of your live URL — username.github.io/repo-name. Pick something professional. Hard to change later.

3

Verify your email

GitHub sends a verification code. Enter it. You're in.

Step 2 — Create a new repo

1

Click + New

Top-right → + → New repository.

2

Name your repo

Use lowercase with hyphens — e.g. my-portfolio or shaid-resume. Spaces become hyphens automatically but pick clean names.

3

Pick "Public"

GitHub Pages only works on public repos for free accounts. Don't worry — there's no requirement to put real personal info in the website itself.

4

Skip the README for now

Leave the boxes unchecked → Create repository. You'll land on an empty repo page with upload instructions.

Step 3 — Upload the index.html Claude built for you

This is the file you saved in Part 5 · Step 5. It should be sitting in your Downloads folder (or wherever you saved it).

1

Click "uploading an existing file"

On the empty repo page you'll see a "or upload an existing file" link. Click it. (Or — Add file dropdown → Upload files.)

2

Drag your index.html in

Drop the file from your Downloads folder onto the upload zone. Wait for the green tick — you should see index.html listed.

3

Commit

Scroll down → leave the commit message as default ("Add files via upload") → Commit changes. Your file is now stored on GitHub.

📁
What's a repo?A folder that lives on GitHub. Each one gets its own URL, version history, and can host a website. Anyone can view it, clone it, fork it. Yours, free, forever.
Part 7 · GitHub Pages

Turn your repo into a live website

GitHub Pages gives every public repo a free public URL. Your index.html becomes a real, share-anywhere website in under 2 minutes.

Step 1 — Open Settings · Pages

1

Inside your repo, click Settings

Top tab on the repo page. Right side. (Not the gear icon in the corner — that's account settings.)

2

Scroll the left sidebar to "Pages"

Under the Code and automation group → click Pages.

Step 2 — Pick the source branch

1

Under "Build and deployment"

Source → keep Deploy from a branch.

2

Branch dropdown → main

Branch → pick main. Folder → leave as /(root).

3

Save

Click Save. GitHub starts building your site immediately.

Step 3 — Share the live URL

1

Wait ~30-60 seconds

Refresh the Pages settings page. A green box appears at the top — "Your site is live at…".

2

Visit the URL

It looks like https://username.github.io/repo-name/. Open it in a new tab — that's your live website.

3

Share it

WhatsApp, LinkedIn, your CV, your email signature. Anyone with the link can see it. No login required for visitors.

📌
The naming ruleThe file must be called exactly index.html (lowercase) and live at the root of the repo. That's the homepage. Add more files later — each becomes a sub-page at username.github.io/repo/about.html, etc.
🔁
Updating your site laterEdit index.html on your computer → upload the new version to the same repo (overwrite) → commit. Pages auto-rebuilds in ~30 seconds. No manual deploy.

Reference

Common mistakes & FAQs

Things that catch students out

❌
Pasting Perplexity answers without citingTeachers can spot AI-written paragraphs in seconds. Always rewrite in your own words and cite the underlying sources Perplexity gave you — not "Perplexity" itself.
❌
Using free Suno songs commerciallyFree-tier songs belong to Suno. Don't upload them to Spotify or use in monetised YouTube videos. Upgrade to Pro ($10) if you need rights.
❌
Naming the file Index.html with a capital IGitHub Pages is case-sensitive. Index.html won't load. Always lowercase index.html.
❌
Making the repo PrivateGitHub Pages only works on public repos for free accounts. Private repos need a paid plan to publish.
❌
Vague prompts to Claude Code"Make me a website" = generic template. "Build a single index.html with hero, 3 project cards, dark theme, accent #ff5d3a, mobile responsive" = the site you actually want.

FAQs

My GitHub Pages site shows 404
Check — (1) repo is Public, (2) file is named exactly index.html, (3) you waited 1 full minute, (4) Settings → Pages source is main + /(root).
Can I use my own domain?
Yes — Settings → Pages → Custom domain. You'll need a domain from Namecheap / GoDaddy / Hostinger first.
Does Claude Code work on phone?
The web version does, technically. But the preview pane is too cramped on mobile — best on a laptop / desktop.
Can I edit my site after publishing?
Yes — re-upload index.html to the same repo. GitHub Pages rebuilds automatically in ~30 sec.
